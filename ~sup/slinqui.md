A dictionary for all my words, phrases, quips and quirks.


## baby boo

### Noun

1. *affectionate*, *endearing* equivalent to “[boo](#boo)”, but specifically describing a baby.
> Oh look it’s a baby boo!

### Etymology

- See [boo](#boo).


## back in a bite

### Remark

1. ‘be right back’ after a meal.
> I gotta have lunch, back in a bite

### Etymology

- *bite* used figuratively to mean a meal

### Notes

- It doesn’t really imply any sort of timeframe – the meal could be really quick, or a full-blown restaurant dinner.
 - Although, “back in” does insinuate you’re intending to return to the conversation.


## back in a blip

### Remark

1. ‘be right back’, but potentially not.
> Got a call, back in a blip

### Etymology

- Reference to [the blip<sup>↗</sup>](https://marvelcinematicuniverse.fandom.com/wiki/Blip).

### Notes

- You never know when a blip might crop up. So you use this phrase to mean you intend to be back very quickly, but might not be if something unexpected happens that you need to deal with.


## boo

### Noun

1. *affectionate*, *endearing* A ‘cute’ term used to describe someone, usually a non-adult.
> Oh you’re such a boo. \*hug*

### Etymology

- From *boo*, a cute word babies canonically tend to make.
- Also influenced by *boo boo*.


## clutch catalyst

### Noun

1. *figurative*, *funny* A cooler name for “adrenaline”. Or “epinephrine”. Both epic names.
> I’m gonna need a lotta clutch catalyst for this test.

### Etymology

- *clutch* refers to ‘rescuing’ an urgent situation.
- *catalyst* is a reference to a [chemical catalyst <sup>↗</sup>](https://wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalysis).
- It’s used figuratively to describe fluid flowing through your body that ‘catalyses clutch’, usually in high-stress situations such as a test, date, or running `sudo rm -rf`.


## faceplunge

### Verb

1. To “plunge” one’s face into something, usually a body part of another person.
> Stop faceplunging into my tummy! Can we hug normally?

### Noun

1. The act of faceplunging.

### Etymology

- *face* + *plunge*


## fingerful

### Adjective

1. Prominently involving or engaging one’s fingers.
> This game is so fingerful.

### Etymology

- *finger* + *-ful*, literal.


## hand acrobatics

### Noun

1. The phenomenon of one’s hands performing finicky, elaborate and potentially impressive movements while engaged in a [fingerful](#fingerful) activity, such as typing or playing rhythm games.
> Typing in *aLtErNaTiNg CaSe* results in such hand acrobatics.

### Etymology

- *hand* + *acrobatics*, literal.


## logistical issue

### Noun

1. Describing a situation or predicament that is difficult to resolve due to issues or obstacles irrelevant to the fundamental problem.
> Getting everyone in a room at the same time for a meeting is such a logistical issue.

### Etymology

- This is a real phrase, but I like to use it to describe very particular situations.

### Notes

- I use the term with negative connotations (sort of exasperation or frustration) since logistical issues often arise in contexts where they’re completely detached from the actual problem.


## *lokay*

### Remark

1. *abbreviation* “lol okay”.
> lokay, sure.

### Etymology

- *lol* + *okay*, literal.

### Notes

- Gotta love abbreviations. It does roll off the tongue quite nicely though!


## productive unproductivity

### Noun

1. *ironic* Working on something that would generally be regarded as inconsequential, especially when you spend a lot of time on it instead of other more pressing or worthwhile endeavours. For instance, writing this dictionary instead of my university applications.
> Unproductive productivity is so much more fun than productive productivity.

### Adjective

as *productively unproductive*
> These past few days have productively unproductive.

### Notes

- See [productivity](../productivity.md) for more.


## *yeup*

### Remark

1. “yep” + “yup”. Reflects a drawn-out “yee-up”, often used for humourous or playful confirmation.
> yeup, we’re screwed.

### Etymology

- *yep* + *yup*, phonetic.

### Notes

- It often sounds indistinguishable from *yup* when said out loud. Drawn-out “y” just sounds like “yeeee”.


## *ytf*

### Remark

1. *acronym* “why the f***”.
> ytf does this taste so delicious

### Etymology

- Following the format of *wtf* for “what the f***”.
- “y” is used literally to be pronounced as `/wai/`.

### Notes

- No one’s seen this, but they know instantly what you mean. iygwim.
