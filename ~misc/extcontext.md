## The Unspoken Rules

The game of *extcontext* rests upon a set of unspoken but paramount axioms. These are the very heart of the game, and without them its character is shattered like glass on a phone screen.

### Never provide more than is necessary
Less is more. The concentration, or density, of hilarity packed into a single screenshot is 

### Never take quotes seriously
As a receiver of out-of-context quotes, do not let their content permanently warp your perception of their author. The game of extcontext is played in good humour. If one insists, the content in quotes should always be assumed sufficiently justifiable, no matter how outlandish or outrageous it would seem. Quotes are not shared to defame or expose (though this can be, and often is, jokingly posited as the intent), but for sh*ts and giggles.

### Never leak the context
Even for the absolute worst. Quotes which seem like no context could possibly justify them.

This is the most vital law of the game, which must be upheld at all costs. Leaking the context is like dissecting a frog: you may finally see the insides of the frog, but it dies – and with it, the entire game is rendered null. One case sets a precedent for future cases, and it becomes increasingly difficult to justify hiding context for future quotes.

As a receiver it is one’s duty to accept the quote without its context, and to inquire about it with the intention of obtaining context is the highest treason.[^treason]

[^treason]: In some domains, this is punishable by silence, exile or doxxation.

## Context is Everything

The game of *extcontext* vividly reveals the importance of *context*.

Even in the apparent absence of context, it is still there – one just has to look further. Out-of-context quotes are shared under the appreciation that they are, indeed, out-of-context quotes.
